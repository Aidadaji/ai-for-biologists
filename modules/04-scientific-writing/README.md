# Module 4 — AI for Scientific Writing

**Duration:** Week 4 (~8-10 hours)
**Outcome:** Draft IMRaD manuscripts, methods sections, and reviewer responses 3× faster — without academic misconduct.

---

## Why this module matters

Writing takes **30-50% of research time** for most biologists. AI can draft in minutes what takes you weeks. But academic integrity rules (UGC, CSIR, ICMR) require:
- Disclosing AI use
- Verifying all citations
- Maintaining authorship responsibility

By end of this module, you'll draft a complete manuscript section + verified bibliography.

## 🇮🇳 India-specific content

### UGC AI use policy (2024 circular)
UGC's guidelines for AI in academic work:
- AI can be used for **language editing** and **grammar checking**
- AI can **NOT** be listed as an author
- AI can **NOT** generate original research content without disclosure
- All AI use must be disclosed in the **Acknowledgments** or **Methods** section
- Failure to disclose = academic misconduct

**Sample disclosure:**
> "Claude (Anthropic, version 3.5) was used for language editing and reference formatting in this manuscript. All scientific content, data interpretation, and conclusions are the authors' own. The authors reviewed and edited all AI-generated text for accuracy."

### CSIR publication guidelines
- CSIR labs have specific publishing norms
- Indian journals (Current Science, J Biosci) often prefer Indian context examples
- Citations should include both Indian and international literature

### ICMR publication ethics (for medical/clinical bio)
- Mandatory clinical trial registration (CTRI) before publication
- Adherence to CONSORT/STROBE/PRISMA guidelines
- AI use must be declared in Methods

### Common Indian writing pitfalls AI can help with
1. **Hindi→English translation** for non-native speakers (Claude/ChatGPT excellent)
2. **Converting bullet points to prose** for manuscript submission
3. **Reducing plagiarism** by paraphrasing while keeping meaning
4. **Formatting references** to journal style

⚠️ **Don't:** Have AI write the entire introduction from scratch. That's academic misconduct.

## ⚠️ Critical warnings

### 🚨 Citation fabrication is rampant
LLMs fabricate 5-20% of citations. **Always verify every reference.**

**The 4-step verification:**
1. Check DOI resolves (https://doi.org/)
2. Check authors + year + journal + volume + pages
3. Read the actual abstract to verify it's about what AI claims
4. Check it's not retracted (retractionwatch.com)

### 🚨 AI over-claims
AI loves to write: "This demonstrates that..."
Reality check: Your data probably "suggests" or "indicates," not "demonstrates"

### 🚨 AI introduces bias
If you ask AI to write a "balanced" discussion, it may:
- Add false balance (where field consensus is strong)
- Cherry-pick studies that support your hypothesis
- Miss contradicting studies

**Fix:** Always provide your own bibliography, then ask AI to organize it.

### 🚨 Plagiarism is sneaky
Even paraphrased AI text can trigger plagiarism detectors (Turnitin now has AI detection).

**Best practice:**
- Use AI for ideas + structure
- Write the prose yourself
- Cite original sources, not AI

## 📚 Writing templates (India-specific)

### Methods section template
```
Animals/Cells:
- [Species, strain, sex, age, source]

Reagents:
- [Catalog #, supplier, lot #]

Equipment:
- [Model, manufacturer]

Procedures:
1. [Step 1 with specific parameters]
2. [Step 2 with specific parameters]
...

Statistical analysis:
- [Software, version, tests used, alpha level]
```

### IMRaD outline (Indian journal style)
```
Title: [Concise, informative, <20 words]
Abstract: [Structured: Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions, 250 words]
Introduction: [Background → Gap → Hypothesis → Objective]
Methods: [Detailed enough to replicate]
Results: [Logical order matching figures]
Discussion: [Key findings → Compare to literature → Limitations → Future work]
Acknowledgments: [Funding, AI use disclosure, technical help]
References: [Vancouver or APA per journal]
```
