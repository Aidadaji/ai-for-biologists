# Module 1 — AI for Literature Review

**Duration:** Week 1 (~8-10 hours)
**Outcome:** Find, read, summarize, and verify research papers 3× faster using AI tools — without getting fooled by hallucinations.

---

## Why this module matters

Biologists spend **30-50% of their research time** reading papers. AI can compress this — but only if you:
1. Know which tools to use for which task
2. Can catch when AI fabricates citations or misrepresents findings
3. Build a verification habit into your workflow

By the end of this module, you'll have a **personal literature matrix** of 10-20 papers on a topic of your choice, with every AI-generated claim verified.

### Lesson 1.2 — Paper Summarization (60 min video)
**The problem:** Most papers are 15-30 pages. You don't have time to read them all deeply.

**You'll learn:**
- The 3-pass reading method (abstract → figures → methods)
- Using AI to extract: hypothesis, methods, key findings, limitations
- Comparing papers side-by-side with AI
- When NOT to trust AI summaries (technical methods, novel statistical tests)

**Hands-on demo:** Summarizing 3 papers in 5 minutes vs 90 minutes manually.

→ **[Lesson 1.2 notes & transcript](./02-paper-summarization.md)**

### Lesson 1.3 — Catching AI Hallucinations ⚠️ CRITICAL (75 min video)
**The problem:** LLMs confidently fabricate citations, gene names, and statistical results. **In biology, this is dangerous.**

**You'll learn:**
- The 5 most common biology hallucinations:
  1. **Fabricated DOIs** (paper doesn't exist)
  2. **Wrong gene names** (made-up symbols that look real)
  3. **Misattributed discoveries** (wrong author/year/journal)
  4. **Invented statistics** (numbers that sound plausible)
  5. **Wrong pathway assignments** (proteins to pathways)
- How to verify each type in 30 seconds
- **India-specific case study:** The Bt brinjal citation error — a real example of how AI summaries can mislead regulatory discussions
- Building the verification habit: always check before you cite

**Hands-on demo:** Catching 5 hallucinations in AI-generated summaries (live).

→ **[Lesson 1.3 notes & transcript](./03-catching-hallucinations.md)**

### Lesson 1.4 — Building a Literature Matrix (45 min video)
**The problem:** After reading 20 papers, you've forgotten what was in paper #7.

**You'll learn:**
- The literature matrix template (Google Sheet or Notion)
- AI-assisted synthesis: "What does the field agree on? Disagree on?"
- Identifying research gaps (where the contradictions are)
- Using AI to generate a 1-page "field overview" from your matrix

**Capstone check:** Your first mini literature review (3-5 papers).

## ⚠️ India-specific tips

1. **Paywalled papers:** Use **INFLIBNET N-LIST** (free for Indian universities) to access journals. Get your university librarian to register you.
2. **Thesis database:** [Shodhganga](https://shodhganga.inflibnet.ac.in/) has 300K+ Indian theses — goldmine for "what's been done in India"
**India-specific:**
- **DBT (Department of Biotechnology)** — https://dbt.gov.in/ (was dbtindia.gov.in)
- **Genome India Project** — now under DBT; access via https://dbt.gov.in/scientific-programmes
- **BTISnet** — now under DBT; access via https://dbt.gov.in/scientific-programmes
- **Indian Biological Data Centre** — https://ibdc.dbtindia.gov.in/ (still working)
4. **NIPER theses:** Available through Shodhganga
5. **Regional journals:** Don't ignore Indian journals (Indian Journal of Biochemistry, JBS, etc.) — AI often misses these
6. **Conference proceedings:** SBC (Society of Biological Chemists), Indian Biophysical Society, etc. — not always indexed

## ❓ Common questions

**Q: My university doesn't have INFLIBNET access. What do I do?**
A: Try Sci-Hub (legality varies by country), ResearchGate (request from authors), or email the corresponding author directly — most will send a PDF within a week.

**Q: Can I use ChatGPT instead of Claude?**
A: Yes. The workflows transfer. Claude is generally better at nuanced biology content; ChatGPT has better image/PDF analysis.

**Q: Should I cite AI in my literature review?**
A: Generally no — AI is a tool, not a source. Cite the papers AI helped you find, not AI itself. (See Module 6 for details.)

**Q: I found a paper that's perfect but it's in Chinese/Russian. Help?**
A: Use NotebookLM to upload it + use Claude or ChatGPT to translate and summarize. Verify the translation by checking key terms in Google Scholar.
