# 🏆 Final Capstone — Choose Your Path

**Duration:** 2 weeks (15-25 hours)
**Outcome:** A finished deliverable you can actually use.

---

## Pick ONE of three paths

### 📚 Path A — Literature Review
**Time:** 15-20 hours
**Best for:** Students starting a project, researchers entering a new field

**Deliverable:**
- 5-page literature review (2000-2500 words) on a topic of your choice
- Bibliography with **20-30 papers**, every citation verified
- **Verification log** showing at least 5 AI hallucinations you caught
- **AI usage log** documenting what you used AI for and how you verified it

**Structure:**
1. Abstract (200 words)
2. Introduction (1 page) — scope, significance
3. Main body (3 pages) — themes, contradictions, gaps
4. Conclusions (1 page) — synthesis, future directions
5. References (1 page) — Vancouver style
6. Appendices: verification log + AI usage log

**Evaluation:** See [grading rubric](./grading-rubric.md)

---

### 📝 Path B — Research Proposal
**Time:** 20-25 hours
**Best for:** Grant applicants, PhD students writing thesis proposals

**Deliverable:**
- 10-page research proposal for a hypothetical project (or your thesis)
- **Hypothesis + aims** clearly stated
- **Methods** with enough detail to evaluate feasibility
- **Expected outcomes + timeline + budget**
- **Verification log + AI usage log** (same as Path A)

**Structure:**
1. Title + 1-paragraph summary
2. Specific Aims (1 page) — 2-3 aims, each with hypothesis + approach
3. Background & Significance (2 pages)
4. Preliminary Data (1 page, can be hypothetical)
5. Research Plan (4 pages) — detailed methods per aim
6. Timeline + Budget (1 page)
7. References (1 page)
8. Appendices: verification log + AI usage log

**Bonus:** Submit as a draft for a real grant (SERB-ECRA, DST INSPIRE, DBT Ramalingaswami)

**Evaluation:** See [grading rubric](./grading-rubric.md)

---

### 📄 Path C — Manuscript Draft
**Time:** 20-25 hours
**Best for:** Researchers with unpublished data, students writing thesis chapters

**Deliverable:**
- Complete IMRaD manuscript draft (Intro + Methods + Results + Discussion)
- **Based on your own unpublished work** OR a hypothetical study
- **All citations verified** (no fabricated DOIs)
- **Verification log + AI usage log** (same as Path A)

**Structure:**
1. Title + Abstract (structured, 250 words)
2. Introduction (2 pages)
3. Methods (3 pages)
4. Results (3 pages, with figure descriptions)
5. Discussion (3 pages)
6. Conclusions (0.5 page)
7. Acknowledgments (with AI disclosure)
8. References (Vancouver style)
9. Appendices: verification log + AI usage log

**Bonus:** Submit to a journal for real peer review (Current Science, J Biosci, etc.)

**Evaluation:** See [grading rubric](./grading-rubric.md)

**Lesson:** Always verify DOI before citing.

## 💡 Tips for capstone success

### Start early
Don't wait until the last week. Module 6 deliverables + capstone take ~30 hours.

### Choose wisely
- **Path A** is easiest (literature only)
- **Path B** is medium (includes hypothetical methods)
- **Path C** is hardest (full manuscript)

Pick the path that matches your actual need.

### Use the verification habits
The capstone is graded partly on your verification log. **The more hallucinations you catch, the better.**

### Peer review is required
You'll review 2 other students' capstones. This teaches you to spot verification issues — useful for your own paper submissions later.

## ❓ Common questions

**Q: Can I submit a real paper I'm working on?**
A: Yes! Path C is designed for this. Use your unpublished data.

**Q: Can I submit work I started before the course?**
A: Yes, but you must show what AI helped with during the course specifically.

**Q: What if my topic is super niche?**
A: That's fine. Niche topics often have cleaner literature.

**Q: What if I find zero hallucinations?**
A: Suspicious. Either you didn't verify enough, or you used AI so carefully that it never hallucinated. Both are unlikely. **Keep looking.**

**Q: Is the capstone graded?**
A: Pass/fail. Pass = you get the certificate. No letter grades.
